Privacy Policy — Tidy Tens

Effective date: 8 November 2026

Contact: tidytens.app@gmail.com

Published by: Elise Reppond

The short version

Tidy Tens has no accounts. Your progress stays on your phone. Three things leave it:

  • Anonymous play statistics: which stages are played, won, lost or abandoned, and how often the app is opened, so the publisher can see where players get stuck and whether they come back. No name, no account, no advertising identifier, no location. You can switch it off in Settings ("Share anonymous play stats"). See "Play statistics" below.
  • When you clear the daily board, that run is sent to the daily leaderboard, under an anonymous id and the name you chose, so it can be ranked against other players' runs. See "The daily leaderboard" below.
  • Ads you choose to watch: a button that names its reward, such as "Watch an ad for 3 hints", shows a short ad from Google AdMob. Google's ad software on the phone talks to Google to fetch it. The ads are not personalised, the app never asks to track you, and in the EU, the UK and Switzerland Google asks for your consent first. See "Advertising" below.

What the app stores

Everything the app saves stays in its own private storage on your device. None of it is transmitted, apart from the play statistics and leaderboard runs described below. Specifically, it keeps:

  • how many stars you have earned on each stage
  • your coin balance
  • how many hints, undos and shuffles you own
  • which stages you have unlocked, and which board you are playing on each one
  • which one-time explanatory pop-ups you have already seen
  • your daily streak: the days you cleared the daily board, any missed day you restored, and which streak rewards you have been given
  • your phone's time-zone offset from UTC (for example "+5:30") when you last cleared the daily board, so a date skipped by flying east does not break your streak. It is a number of minutes, not a place, and like everything here it never leaves the phone
  • whether you turned on the daily reminder
  • whether you share anonymous play statistics
  • in the EU, the UK and Switzerland, your answer to Google's consent form, kept on the phone by Google's consent software so it is not asked again
  • whether developer/testing mode is switched on, which is a device-only setting

None of that identifies you. There is no email, no account, no contact information, no location, and no advertising identifier. The app itself has no crash or diagnostic reporting; Google's ad software reports its own (see "Advertising"). The name you type is only on your phone, unless you clear a daily board (below).

What the app does not do

  • It does not create an account or ask you to sign in. The leaderboard uses an anonymous id made for your install, never an account.
  • It never shows an ad you did not choose: no banners, no ad when the app opens, and none in the middle of a stage.
  • It does not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and it never reads your phone's advertising identifier, so it never shows Apple's tracking prompt.
  • It does not include crash reporting of its own.
  • It has two third-party SDKs that send anything: Google's ad SDK, for the ads you choose, and PostHog's analytics SDK, set up to send only the play statistics below.
  • It does not request access to your contacts, photos, camera, microphone, or location.
  • It asks permission to show notifications only if you turn on the daily reminder. The reminder is set and shown by your phone itself; nothing is sent anywhere to make it happen, and you can turn it off in the app's Settings or your phone's.
  • It makes no network requests except to send play statistics (while that is switched on), to send a cleared daily board to the leaderboard, to show the day's top runs on the Daily screen, and for the ads (Google's consent check, and fetching an ad so one is ready when you ask).

Deleting your data

Everything the app stores on your phone goes when you delete the app. Play statistics cannot be traced back to you once the app is deleted, because the random id they were sent under goes with it; switching them off in Settings stops them at once. Runs you sent to the daily leaderboard are deleted with Settings → "Delete my leaderboard data": do that before deleting the app, because afterwards nothing ties those runs to you any more. You can also write to the contact address above. Your answer to Google's consent form goes with the app; what Google's ad software sent is held by Google under its own policy, and the publisher has no copy of it.

Children

Tidy Tens is made for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13. The app asks for no age and no personal details. The only thing other players can see is the name and scores on the daily leaderboard; names are filtered (rude words, links, @handles and official-looking names show as "Player"), and the publisher removes names that are reported. Its ads are limited to ones Google rates suitable for a general audience with parental guidance ("PG"), and are never personalised. It has no links out, other than those inside an ad, and no way to message anyone.

Apple and Google

Apple, or Google on Android, may collect its own information about downloads and purchases, under its own privacy policy rather than this one. That is between you and the store; the app does not receive it.

Play statistics

While "Share anonymous play stats" is on in Settings (it starts on), the app sends short reports about play to PostHog, an analytics service, so the publisher can see which stages are too hard, where players stop, and whether they come back the next day and the next week. Switch it off and nothing more is sent.

What is sent:

  • a random id made for this install and kept on the phone, so that a return visit can be counted (not your name, email, Apple ID, the phone's advertising identifier or Apple's identifier for vendors)
  • what happened, as names and numbers only: the app opened or went to the background and for how long; a stage started, won, lost or left, with its stage and board number, the mode, stars, adds used and seconds taken; the board getting stuck and what you chose next; adds, hints running out, and taps that just missed a matching number; coins spent in the shop; daily streak restores, milestones and the reminder switch; rewarded-ad buttons shown and pressed
  • whether testing mode was on
  • the app's version, the phone's operating system and its version, whether it is a phone or a tablet, and the phone's language

What is not sent: your name, the boards or your moves, anything you type, the phone's name, model or maker, its time zone, its location (location lookup from the network address is switched off), contacts, photos, or anything else on the phone. No crash reports, no screen recordings, no record of taps beyond the events above.

Where it goes: PostHog's EU cloud (eu.i.posthog.com), stored in the European Union, in a project run by the publisher. PostHog's own terms and privacy policy apply to the hosting.

What it is used for: improving the game: difficulty, the first few minutes, and which features are used. Nothing else: no profile of you is built (person profiles are switched off), nothing is used for advertising, and nothing is sold or shared.

Not tracking: the statistics are never linked to your identity or combined with other companies' data, so under Apple's rules they are not "tracking" and no tracking prompt is shown.

Advertising

Some buttons offer a reward for watching an ad: an extra add when you are stuck, hints when you have none, double coins for a win, and a few others. Each says what you get. Nothing else in the app shows an ad, and you can play every stage and clear every board without watching one.

Who shows them: Google AdMob, run by Google. Its software (the Google Mobile Ads SDK and Google's consent SDK) is built into the app and talks to Google directly, under Google's privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). How Google uses data from apps that show its ads: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

Consent first: in the EU (and the rest of the EEA), the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form appears the first time the app opens, before any ad is fetched. You can change your answer at any time in Settings → "Privacy and cookie settings". Elsewhere no form is shown.

Not personalised: every ad request asks Google for non-personalised ads, so ads are not chosen from a profile of you or your activity in other apps. The app never reads your phone's advertising identifier and never shows Apple's tracking prompt. Google still uses some data for non-personalised ads, for example to choose an ad from the app and your general location, to limit how often you see the same ad, to count views and to prevent fraud.

What Google's software collects, by Google's own description: your IP address, used to estimate your general location; identifiers that only work within this app or apps from the same publisher (not the advertising identifier); the ads shown and how you interacted with them (views, taps); app launches and similar interaction data; and its own performance and crash data.

When it is sent: Google's consent check runs each time the app opens. So that an ad is ready the moment you ask, the app fetches one from Google in the background while you play; nothing is shown unless you press the button. While the game's sound is off, ads play muted.

What the publisher receives: only totals from Google (how many ads were shown and what they earned), never anything about you. The app's play statistics record that an ad button was shown, and whether an ad was watched or skipped (see "Play statistics"), but nothing about the ad itself.

The daily leaderboard

When you clear the daily board, the app sends that run to a server so it can be listed against other players' runs. Nothing else is sent: the chapters, the modes and your progress stay on your phone. Settings has a "Delete my leaderboard data" button.

What is sent, and only then:

  • an anonymous id made for your install (a random string; not your name, email, phone number, Apple ID or device identifier)
  • the name you typed in Settings, if any; otherwise "Player". A name the filter refuses (rude words, links, @handles, names that look official) is listed as "Player", and Settings tells you so as you type it
  • the day's board number, your score, how many adds you used, how long you took, and the list of moves you made, which the server replays to check the score is real

Where it goes: a Firebase project (Google Cloud) run by the publisher, in the United States (nam5). Firebase's own terms and privacy policy apply to the hosting; the publisher does not use Firebase Analytics, advertising features or any other Google service with this data.

What it keeps from it: your best run of the day (name, score, adds, time and its moves), how many clean runs of the board you cleared that day, and a short code worked out from each counted run's moves and time, so a run sent twice is counted once.

What it is used for: ranking the day's board and showing it in the app. Nothing else: no profiling, no advertising, no sharing with anyone.

What other players see: your listed name, score, adds and time, and how many times you cleared the board that day ("3 clears"), on the day's board. Never the id, never the moves.

Deleting it: Settings → "Delete my leaderboard data" removes every run you have sent, at once. Deleting the app also abandons the anonymous id, so nothing sent later could be tied to you, but the runs already sent stay on the board until you delete them from within the app first.

App Privacy answers for a version with the leaderboard on: Data collected, linked to you: User ID (the anonymous id) and Gameplay Content (name, scores, moves), for App Functionality; not used for tracking. Play statistics add their own answers, under "Notes for Elise" below.

Changes to this policy

Two more changes are planned, and neither has shipped:

  • Cloud saves, so progress survives a new phone. That would mean storing the same game progress listed above on a server.
  • Ads between stages, from the same Google service.

If either arrives, this policy will be updated and the change described before the version that includes it is released. Nothing in the app today does either.
